\chapter{Methodology}
\label{chap:methodology}

This chapter presents the solution approach adopted in the thesis. It opens with the high-level research design and pipeline, then steps through the study universe and data sources, news collection and article preparation, bilingual sentiment extraction, formal definitions of confidence filtering and daily aggregation, event-aware variants, target construction and lagged alignment, the forecasting framework, evaluation metrics, leakage control, and the four auxiliary experiment protocols. Numbered formulas are introduced wherever they govern reproducible behaviour in the pipeline and are referenced by number in the prose that follows.

\section{Research Design}

This thesis is carried out as an industrial bachelor project between the German University in Cairo and Robin, the data-science arm of Beltone Holding. The original project brief asks whether sentiment information provides additional predictive value beyond historical market data in financial forecasting. The practical work evolves in two stages.

The first stage extends an existing EGX direction-forecasting system that already uses historical prices, engineered technical indicators, and machine-learning models. In exploratory development, the addition of a sentiment signal appears to improve directional accuracy by a small absolute margin. That result is useful as motivation, but it does not isolate the value of sentiment itself because the underlying price-based model is already strong and heavily feature-engineered.

The second stage, which forms the core reproducible contribution of this thesis, reframes the problem as a sentiment-only benchmark. The goal is a narrower and more defensible question: if price-derived predictors are removed, how much information does bilingual financial-news sentiment alone provide about future stock direction and return? This design makes the interpretation cleaner. If the sentiment-only benchmark is weak, the study can report that result directly. If it is moderately useful, later integration with price-based systems becomes easier to justify.

The final methodology evaluates three complementary ideas. The first is bilingual sentiment extraction from Arabic and English financial news. The second is leakage-safe alignment of article-derived features to later trading dates. The third is the comparison of all-news, event-only, and event-weighted sentiment representations. Figure~\ref{fig:methodology_pipeline} summarises the end-to-end workflow adopted in the thesis, from bilingual news collection to leakage-safe evaluation.

\section{Study Universe and Data Sources}

The empirical study focuses on three Egyptian Exchange tickers: Commercial International Bank, also written as CIB or COMI; Talaat Moustafa Group, also written as TMG or TMGH; and Orascom Investment Holding, also written as OIH. These tickers are selected because they are actively discussed in local financial coverage and provide a mix of relatively higher-coverage and lower-coverage cases.

The final reproducible benchmark uses Mubasher as the sole news source because it offers both Arabic and English listing pages tied to specific EGX company pages. Restricting the thesis dataset to one source keeps crawling behaviour, ticker linkage, and article structure consistent across the study. Historical market prices are loaded from local CSV files stored in the project repository, with external download used only as a fallback if a local file is missing.

\section{News Collection and Article Preparation}

The news pipeline begins by scraping Mubasher listing pages separately for Arabic and English. For each ticker-language pair, the crawler collects article identifiers, URLs, titles, summaries, and article bodies. The crawler is incremental and retry-aware so that interrupted collection can be resumed without restarting the archive.

Collected articles pass through a preparation stage that standardises company references and removes low-quality or duplicated records. The preparation stage resolves ticker aliases to a canonical identity, normalises timestamps to Cairo local time, cleans Arabic and English text, removes very short or duplicated records, and filters weak entity matches. The result is a sentiment-ready bilingual article table whose rows correspond to distinct company-linked news items.

Article relevance is then represented explicitly rather than assumed. A direct article contains the company alias in the title. A contextual article contains the alias in the body but not in the title. An indirect article is linked through weaker alias evidence. A stock-page-only article appears on the company page without stronger textual support. These categories are converted into weights so that strong company references contribute more than weak ones when daily sentiment features are aggregated.

\section{Bilingual Sentiment Extraction}

After article preparation, sentiment is scored using separate models for English and Arabic. English articles are routed to FinBERT \cite{araci2019finbert}, while Arabic articles are routed to an AraBERT-based sentiment route \cite{antoun2020arabert}. The reason for routing by language instead of forcing both languages through one shared model is methodological. The literature reviewed in Chapter~\ref{chap:related} shows that domain-specific financial English models and Arabic-specialised language models are both more defensible than generic multilingual sentiment tools for their respective settings.

For each article, the sentiment layer stores the detected language, the model route, the predicted label, the class probabilities, a model-confidence estimate, and a continuous sentiment score. The article-level sentiment contribution is then weighted by both ticker-match confidence and model confidence. This design downweights weak ticker matches and low-confidence predictions before any daily aggregation takes place.

The use of off-the-shelf transformer models is a pragmatic research choice rather than a claim that these models are optimal for Egyptian financial language. The thesis objective is a test of whether a language-aware bilingual sentiment signal exists at all under a conservative benchmark. If such a signal exists, later work can fine-tune these models on financial-domain Arabic and English corpora.

\section{Confidence Filtering and Daily Sentiment Aggregation}

Article-level predictions are converted into time-series features through two formal steps: an entity-match confidence filter, then a confidence-weighted daily aggregation.

\paragraph{Entity-match confidence filter.}
Each candidate article $i$ carries a categorical ticker-match confidence $c_i \in \{\text{low}, \text{medium}, \text{high}\}$ produced by the matcher in \texttt{src/news\_pipeline}. Let $\mathrm{rank}(\cdot)$ assign $\mathrm{rank}(\text{low})=0$, $\mathrm{rank}(\text{medium})=1$, $\mathrm{rank}(\text{high})=2$. Article $i$ is retained for daily aggregation if and only if
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{rank}(c_i) \geq \mathrm{rank}(\tau), \quad \tau = \text{medium},
\label{eq:confidence_filter}
\end{equation}
where the threshold $\tau$ is taken from the saved benchmark configuration\footnote{Default \texttt{min\_match\_confidence} in \texttt{SentimentOnlyForecastConfig} inside \texttt{src/egx\_direction/sentiment\_only\_pipeline.py}.}. Equivalent to (\ref{eq:confidence_filter}), low-confidence matches are dropped before any sentiment score is aggregated.

\paragraph{Article weighting.}
Each retained article $i$ contributes a non-negative aggregation weight
\begin{equation}
w_i = w^{\text{rel}}_{r(i)} \cdot w^{\text{match}}_{c_i} \cdot p_i \cdot \mathbf{1}[\text{is\_scored}_i = 1],
\label{eq:article_weight}
\end{equation}
where $r(i) \in \{\text{direct},\text{contextual},\text{indirect},\text{stock\_page\_only}\}$ is the entity-relevance label of article $i$ and $p_i \in [0,1]$ is the model confidence for the predicted sentiment class. The fixed relevance and match weights are $w^{\text{rel}} = (1.00, 0.85, 0.65, 0.45)$ and $w^{\text{match}} = (0.60, 0.80, 1.00)$ for $c_i \in (\text{low}, \text{medium}, \text{high})$. After the filter in (\ref{eq:confidence_filter}), only $c_i \in \{\text{medium}, \text{high}\}$ contribute to (\ref{eq:article_weight}).

\paragraph{Article sign.}
The article-level sign $s_i$ derives from the predicted sentiment label $\ell_i$ as
\begin{equation}
s_i =
\begin{cases}
+1, & \ell_i = \text{positive} \\
-1, & \ell_i = \text{negative} \\
\phantom{+}0, & \ell_i = \text{neutral}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:article_sign}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Daily weighted sentiment.}
For ticker $s$ and source calendar day $d-1$, the daily weighted sentiment is
\begin{equation}
S_{s,d} =
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}_{s,d-1}} s_i \cdot w_i}{\sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}_{s,d-1}} w_i}, & \text{if } \sum_{i \in \mathcal{A}_{s,d-1}} w_i > 0 \\
0, & \text{otherwise (missingness flag set to }1)
\end{cases}
\label{eq:daily_aggregation}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{A}_{s,d-1}$ is the set of retained articles linked to ticker $s$ whose Cairo-local publication date equals $d-1$. The window therefore lags the trading date $d$ by one calendar day so that no same-day article is seen at prediction time. This corresponds to the configured value $\texttt{sentiment\_lag\_calendar\_days}=1$ in the saved \texttt{run\_config.json} of the sentiment-stock-impact study and to \texttt{lag\_strategy=previous\_day} (one-day shift) in the news-pipeline aggregator.

\paragraph{Daily missingness handling.}
Equation (\ref{eq:daily_aggregation}) sets $S_{s,d} = 0$ when the denominator vanishes. The pipeline also records a binary \emph{article\_has\_news} indicator so that downstream models can distinguish a genuine zero-sentiment reading from a no-article day. Rolling windows and recency indicators derived later in the pipeline are computed on the daily series obtained from (\ref{eq:daily_aggregation}) after the one-day shift in (\ref{eq:article_weight}) and (\ref{eq:daily_aggregation}).

Three language views are retained throughout the experiments: an English-only view ($\mathcal{A}^{\text{en}}_{s,d-1}$), an Arabic-only view ($\mathcal{A}^{\text{ar}}_{s,d-1}$), and a bilingual view that combines both languages after routing and scoring. This separation matters because the Egyptian information environment is genuinely bilingual. The experiments therefore test whether combining both languages adds signal or whether it adds noise.

\section{Event-Aware Feature Variants}

In addition to standard daily sentiment aggregation, the thesis evaluates event-aware features. Articles differ not only in polarity but also in event type, and not all events are equally likely to move a stock. The final pipeline labels articles using broad market-moving categories such as earnings, dividends, mergers and acquisitions, contracts and projects, expansion and investment, financing, and governance or disclosure.

These labels support three feature strategies. The all-news strategy keeps every retained article in the daily aggregation in (\ref{eq:daily_aggregation}). The event-only strategy restricts the aggregation set $\mathcal{A}_{s,d-1}$ to event-related articles. The event-weighted strategy keeps all retained articles but multiplies the weight in (\ref{eq:article_weight}) by an additional event factor for event-related rows. This is not a full thematic NLP model, but it is a practical intermediate step toward richer event-aware relevance modelling.

\section{Target Construction and Lagged Alignment}

The target variables derive from future closing prices. For each trading day $t$ and forecast horizon $h$, the future return is defined as
\begin{equation}
r_{t,h} = \frac{P_{t+h}}{P_t} - 1,
\label{eq:return}
\end{equation}
where $P_t$ is the closing price on day $t$ and $P_{t+h}$ is the closing price after $h$ future trading days. A binary direction target is then defined as
\begin{equation}
y_{t,h}^{\text{dir}} =
\begin{cases}
1, & r_{t,h} > 0 \\
0, & r_{t,h} \leq 0
\end{cases}
\label{eq:direction}
\end{equation}

The thesis evaluates three short forecast horizons, reported with the corresponding implementation settings in Chapter~\ref{chap:results}.

Temporal alignment is one of the most important parts of the methodology. The daily sentiment $S_{s,d}$ in (\ref{eq:daily_aggregation}) is constructed from the prior calendar day, so the features available for trading day $t$ contain only news published before that trading date. This conservative lag is essential for avoiding subtle look-ahead effects and matches the saved configuration constants quoted above.

\section{Forecasting Framework}

The forecasting layer is intentionally simple because the objective is to isolate the informational value of sentiment rather than to maximise performance through model complexity. The thesis therefore uses one low-capacity classifier for direction and one low-capacity regressor for future return. Feature standardisation, model selection, and classification-threshold selection are all performed chronologically on validation data only.

Each ticker panel is sorted by date and divided into chronological training, validation, and test regions. Adjacent regions are separated by an embargo at least as long as the forecast horizon, which prevents overlapping target windows from leaking across splits. Final predictions are generated in an expanding-window walk-forward scheme. At every step, the model is trained only on rows whose target values are already known at the prediction date. This procedure is slower than a random split, but it is more credible. The exact hyperparameter values appear in Chapter~\ref{chap:results} together with the experimental setup.

\section{Evaluation Metrics}

The study reports both classification and regression metrics because the task has two outputs: future direction and future return. Directional evaluation uses accuracy, balanced accuracy, precision, recall, F1, and confusion-matrix counts. Return evaluation uses mean absolute error, root mean squared error, return sign accuracy, and the correlation between predicted and realised returns.

Balanced accuracy is treated as a primary metric rather than as an auxiliary one because the direction labels are not expected to be perfectly balanced across all ticker-horizon combinations. A model that leans too strongly toward the majority direction can still appear acceptable under raw accuracy alone. The reported metric set is therefore designed to expose that behaviour rather than to conceal it.

\section{Leakage Control and Validity Checks}

Leakage is a common failure mode in financial forecasting, so the final pipeline includes explicit validity checks beyond the main experiment loop. These checks verify that the historical rows used for any validation or test prediction do not extend into the future target window of the evaluation row. The checks also inspect source coverage and compare training, validation, and test behaviour, which prevents acceptance of suspiciously optimistic results without further scrutiny.

The overall intention of the methodology is conservative. The thesis prefers a weaker but believable result over an inflated result produced by loose alignment, optimistic evaluation, or metric choice. That design choice is especially important in an emerging-market setting with limited bilingual data.

\section{Auxiliary Experiment Protocols}

In addition to the final sentiment-only benchmark, the thesis specifies four auxiliary experiments that explain how the research process evolves and where future work should go. These experiments are not used to replace the accepted final benchmark. Their role is to test surrounding claims and to prevent undocumented exploratory work from being reported informally.

The first auxiliary experiment returns to the original industrial motivation. An external EGX direction-forecasting repository developed during the Robin/Beltone internship is re-run as a historical price-based baseline. The same chronological train-test split is used for each ticker-model pair, and the baseline feature set is compared against an augmented version that adds one-day-lagged bilingual sentiment features. This design tests whether sentiment adds value inside a richer market-feature model, but it is treated as exploratory because the target formulation and feature regime differ from the stricter sentiment-only benchmark.

The second auxiliary experiment evaluates direct sentiment-return association. Daily bilingual sentiment is shifted by one calendar day, aligned to later trading dates, and correlated with same-day, 1-day, 3-day, and 5-day returns. Pearson and Spearman correlations are reported together with five-day block-permutation p-values. This experiment is descriptive rather than causal. Its purpose is to check whether the raw sentiment score has a stable monotonic relationship with later returns before more complex forecasting models are interpreted.

The third auxiliary experiment tests Arabic financial-domain adaptation. The Arabic article corpus is used for Masked Language Modeling adaptation of AraBERT, following the first stage of the FinBERT-style domain-adaptation logic. The adapted checkpoint is then evaluated against the original AraBERT model on held-out Arabic financial text under identical conditions: the same chronological 80/20 validation split, the same fixed seed of 42 across Python, NumPy, and PyTorch, the same base AraBERT tokenizer for both runs, and the identical evaluation script. This is a language-modelling evaluation, not a sentiment classification benchmark, and is therefore reported as a domain language modelling fit measurement rather than as supervised sentiment performance. A supervised Arabic financial sentiment model requires independent human-labeled examples, which are not available in the current thesis dataset.

The fourth auxiliary experiment evaluates social-media feasibility. The target is a free, ethical, and officially approved route for adding social-media data. Reddit and X/Twitter are considered as candidate sources. The protocol rejects unofficial scraping and paid-access bypasses. Social media is therefore kept as future work unless approved official access is obtained and the resulting data can be handled without redistributing raw user content.

\section{Chapter Summary}

This chapter described the solution approach adopted in the thesis. The method begins with bilingual Mubasher news collection, converts cleaned company-linked articles into language-aware sentiment scores, formalises the entity-match confidence filter and the confidence-weighted daily aggregation in equations (\ref{eq:confidence_filter})--(\ref{eq:daily_aggregation}), aligns the resulting daily features conservatively to later trading dates through the lag in (\ref{eq:daily_aggregation}), and evaluates their forecasting value under chronological walk-forward validation. The chapter also defined the auxiliary experiment protocols used to document historical-model integration, sentiment-return association, Arabic financial-domain adaptation, and social-media feasibility. The next chapter reports the concrete implementation settings and the empirical results obtained from this design.
