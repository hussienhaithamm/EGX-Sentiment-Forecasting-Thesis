\chapter{Methodology}
\label{chap:methodology}

This chapter explains the method used in the thesis. The motivation is to isolate bilingual financial-news sentiment before adding any price-based information, so the method starts from historical EGX30 membership, collects bilingual company-linked news, routes text to suitable sentiment models, builds daily market mood, constructs EGX30 direction labels, and evaluates the result with walk-forward validation.

\section{Overall Pipeline Architecture}

The method isolates the news signal before adding any price-based information. Historical EGX30 closes are used only after feature construction, and only to define future direction labels. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline_architecture} shows the end-to-end flow.

The ordering in Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline_architecture} is important. Articles are filtered against the point-in-time universe before aggregation, and sentiment features are lagged before they are aligned to trading dates. This prevents same-day information and future labels from entering the prediction procedure.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:sentiment_panel} summarizes the construction of the final sentiment feature panel. The important constraint is that prices enter only after feature construction, and only to define the direction labels.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Sentiment Feature Panel Construction}
\label{alg:sentiment_panel}
\small
\textbf{Input:} point-in-time EGX30 registry, article table, sentiment scores, trading calendar, EGX30 close series for labels only, and forecast horizons $H=\{1,3,5\}$.\\
\textbf{Output:} lagged sentiment-only feature panels and direction labels.
\begin{enumerate}
  \setlength{\itemsep}{0.15em}
  \item Normalize article timestamps to Cairo local dates.
  \item Remove articles outside the study period.
  \item Deduplicate repeated article records.
  \item Keep an article only when its linked company is active in EGX30 on the article date.
  \item Route text by language.
  \item Score English text with FinBERT and Arabic text with AraBERT or CAMeLBERT.
  \item Aggregate scored articles to company-day sentiment.
  \item Aggregate active company-day sentiment to equal-weight daily market mood.
  \item Build sentiment-side coverage and count features.
  \item Shift all sentiment features by at least one calendar day.
  \item Align shifted features to trading dates.
  \item Construct EGX30 direction labels from future closes only.
  \item Remove all price-derived fields from the model matrix.
  \item Return the feature panel for walk-forward evaluation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{algorithm}

The output of Algorithm~\ref{alg:sentiment_panel} is the only input used by the forecasting models. Because all sentiment features are shifted before trading-date alignment, the model cannot use same-day or future information.

\section{Point-in-Time EGX30 Universe}

Using the current EGX30 list for past dates would introduce survivorship bias. The study therefore reconstructs the active constituent set from 21 April 2021 through 20 April 2026. The registry contains 12 continuous intervals and 57 historical company identities.

\section{Bilingual Article Collection and Preprocessing}

The news corpus is built from Arabic and English Mubasher company pages linked to the historical EGX30 constituent union. Each article is normalized to a Cairo local publication date. Rows outside the study period are removed, duplicate article records are collapsed, and article text is cleaned before sentiment scoring.

Preprocessing also preserves audit fields such as source language, detected language, article identifier, URL, company ticker, and publication timestamp. These fields make the final corpus reproducible and support later checks for membership validity and cross-company duplication.

\section{Sentiment Scoring}

The scoring step routes English articles to FinBERT and Arabic articles to Arabic transformer encoders. The main Arabic route uses AraBERT, and the comparison route uses CAMeLBERT. The English route is kept fixed so that differences between the two bilingual sentiment sources mainly reflect the Arabic encoder choice.

For article $i$, let $s_i$ be the continuous sentiment score and $w_i$ be the article weight from relevance and confidence. Equation~\ref{eq:market_article_weight} defines the weighted article contribution:
\begin{equation}
q_i = s_i w_i.
\label{eq:market_article_weight}
\end{equation}
This contribution is still a text-derived quantity. It does not use prices, returns, or trading volume.

\section{Market-Mood Feature Construction}

Article sentiment is first aggregated at the company-day level. For company $c$ on calendar day $d$, Equation~\ref{eq:company_sentiment} defines the daily company signal:
\begin{equation}
S_{c,d} = \frac{\sum_{i \in A_{c,d}} q_i}{\sum_{i \in A_{c,d}} w_i},
\label{eq:company_sentiment}
\end{equation}
where $A_{c,d}$ is the set of scored articles linked to company $c$ on day $d$. If a company has no scored article on that day, its sentiment contribution is set to zero for equal-weight market aggregation.

For the active EGX30 constituent set $C_d$, Equation~\ref{eq:pit_market_mood} gives the daily market-mood feature:
\begin{equation}
M_d = \frac{1}{|C_d|} \sum_{c \in C_d} S_{c,d}.
\label{eq:pit_market_mood}
\end{equation}
This equal-weight design prevents heavily covered companies from dominating the index-level signal. Coverage counts, language counts, and constituent coverage ratios are retained as sentiment-side features because they describe the availability of the news signal.

All daily sentiment features are shifted by at least one calendar day before alignment to trading dates. News observed on calendar day $d$ can therefore affect a prediction no earlier than the next available trading session.

\section{EGX30 Target Construction}

EGX30 closes are used only for label construction. For trading day $t$ and horizon $h \in \{1,3,5\}$, Equation~\ref{eq:return} defines the future return:
\begin{equation}
r_{t,h} = \frac{P_{t+h}}{P_t} - 1,
\label{eq:return}
\end{equation}
where $P_t$ is the EGX30 close on trading day $t$. Equation~\ref{eq:direction} gives the binary direction label:
\begin{equation}
y_{t,h} = \mathbf{1}[r_{t,h} > 0].
\label{eq:direction}
\end{equation}
The close values, returns, and horizon-end dates are excluded from the feature matrix. This keeps the model sentiment-only and avoids converting the thesis into a price-based forecasting study.

\section{Baselines and Model Evaluation}

The primary direction model is logistic regression with standardized sentiment features. Ridge regression is retained only as a secondary return-oriented diagnostic in the repository; the thesis interpretation focuses on direction classification.

The direction model is compared with always-Up, always-Down, training-majority, rolling-majority, article-count-only, and shuffled-sentiment baselines. The always-class baselines expose label skew. The majority baselines test whether the model does more than follow historical class balance. The article-count-only baseline tests whether news volume alone explains performance. The shuffled-sentiment sanity check breaks sentiment timing while preserving the evaluation path.

The main metrics are accuracy, balanced accuracy, F1, MCC, actual Up share, predicted Up share, and delta versus the training-majority baseline. Balanced accuracy is prioritized because it is less likely than raw accuracy to reward majority-class prediction.

\section{Supplementary Historical XGBoost Integration}

After the sentiment-only benchmark is established, a supplementary integration experiment tests whether the same lagged market-mood signal improves a historical EGX30 forecasting model. This experiment uses the public EGX-Direction-Forecasting implementation by \texttt{anastamerr} as the historical-model reference and evaluates only its XGBoost classifier for the thesis discussion.

The historical-only variant uses Mubasher EGX30 Open, High, Low, Close, Volume (OHLCV) data from 21 April 2016 through 20 April 2026. The external preprocessor builds price-derived historical features from this series. The augmented variant keeps the identical historical feature matrix and appends 49 one-day-lagged and rolling bilingual market-mood sentiment features from the thesis pipeline. Both variants therefore share the same target definition, train/test split, scaling path, and test labels.

This supplementary experiment is intentionally separated from the main sentiment-only evaluation. Its target follows the historical-model repository: a positive class is assigned when the future 5-session average price is above the past 10-session average price. Rows whose future-window target cannot be observed are removed before scoring. The split is chronological, with a gap of 15 rows between train and test to cover the future window and return lags.

\section{Leakage-Safe Validation and Robustness Checks}

Panels are sorted chronologically and split into training, validation, and test regions with an embargo at least as long as the forecast horizon. Hyperparameters and classification thresholds are selected on validation predictions only. Validation and test predictions use expanding walk-forward histories limited to rows whose target labels would already be observable.

The validity audit recomputes targets from pinned EGX30 closes, verifies one-day lagging, checks active membership, checks cross-company deduplication, enforces walk-forward history limits, and recomputes saved metrics from predictions. Additional robustness reports summarize coverage concentration, class imbalance, baseline comparisons, abstention on high-signal days, sector aggregation scaffolding, and the unavailability of true historical index weights.

\section{Chapter Summary}

This chapter described the thesis method as a single reproducible workflow. The key design choice is to test bilingual financial-news sentiment without allowing price-derived predictors into the model matrix. Point-in-time membership, lagged aggregation, walk-forward evaluation, and validity audits are built into the design rather than added after the fact. The next chapter reports the empirical results.
