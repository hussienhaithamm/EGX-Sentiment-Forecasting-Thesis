\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:intro}

Financial markets allocate capital, transfer risk, and react continuously to public information. When listed firms release news or when financial media reports new developments, investors update expectations about value and risk. For analysts and research desks, the practical question is not simply whether prices move. It is whether public information can be processed in a disciplined way before its relation to future market direction disappears.

Short-horizon forecasting is difficult because market reactions are noisy and unstable. Public information can be absorbed quickly, and the same type of news can affect market regimes differently. The efficient-market literature explains why persistent predictability is hard to find, while empirical forecasting studies show that weak and conditional patterns can still exist under carefully defined targets and validation protocols \cite{fama1970, lo1988randomwalk, sezer2020dlreview}.

Financial news is relevant because it contains semantic information that price histories do not directly encode. A price series records that a movement occurred, but it does not explain whether the movement followed earnings news, financing plans, governance changes, regulation, or contracts. Textual sentiment is therefore useful as a research signal because it tests whether the tone and relevance of public news contain incremental information beyond numerical market history \cite{tetlock2007media, kearney2014survey}.

Most sentiment-forecasting work focuses on developed markets, English text, social media, or models that combine text with price-derived predictors. Those settings are useful but do not directly answer the Egyptian-market question. The Egyptian Exchange is a bilingual Arabic-English information environment, and the EGX30 index provides a broad market target that is more appropriate for market-mood aggregation than a small set of selected equities.

This thesis studies a narrow problem: whether bilingual financial-news sentiment from point-in-time EGX30 constituents contains short-horizon directional information for the EGX30 index. The feature matrix excludes historical prices, EGX30 closes are used only to build labels, and balanced accuracy is treated as more important than raw accuracy because the direction labels are imbalanced.

\section{Problem Statement}

There is limited reproducible evidence on whether Arabic-English financial-news sentiment from the historical EGX30 constituent universe contains short-horizon directional information for the EGX30 index. Existing work often uses English-only data, current constituent lists, price-derived predictors, or evaluation protocols that are difficult to audit. This creates a gap for an Egyptian-market study that combines point-in-time market coverage, bilingual sentiment processing, sentiment-only predictors, and strict walk-forward validation.

\section{Motivation}

The main motivation is methodological clarity. If sentiment is mixed immediately with prices, returns, volume, or technical indicators, it becomes difficult to tell whether text itself contributes useful information. A sentiment-only benchmark isolates the textual signal first. Later price-plus-sentiment models can be evaluated more fairly only after this baseline is understood.

The second motivation is local relevance. EGX-related news is not purely English and not purely Arabic. A method that ignores either language loses part of the information environment, while mixing both languages without routing can introduce avoidable noise. Comparing Arabic encoder choices is part of the core study, not a separate side experiment.

The third motivation is reproducibility. The EGX30 changes over time, so using the current index composition to collect past news would bias the study toward firms that survived into the present list. Reconstructing the point-in-time constituent universe makes the corpus and the market target consistent.

\section{Research Questions}

\noindent\textbf{RQ1.} Does bilingual financial-news sentiment from point-in-time EGX30 constituents contain short-horizon directional information for the EGX30 index?

\noindent\textbf{RQ2.} How do Arabic sentiment encoder choice and forecast horizon affect sentiment-only EGX30 direction classification performance?

\noindent\textbf{RQ3.} Do any observed results remain meaningful under leakage-safe walk-forward validation, class-imbalance diagnostics, and stronger baseline comparisons?

\section{Objectives}

\noindent\textbf{Objective 1.} Reconstruct a point-in-time EGX30 constituent universe for the study period.

\noindent\textbf{Objective 2.} Collect, normalize, and score Arabic and English financial-news articles linked to historical EGX30 constituents.

\noindent\textbf{Objective 3.} Construct sentiment-only daily market-mood features while excluding all price-derived predictors from the model matrix.

\noindent\textbf{Objective 4.} Define 1-, 3-, and 5-trading-day EGX30 direction labels using historical closes only for target construction.

\noindent\textbf{Objective 5.} Evaluate sentiment-only forecasting performance under strict expanding-window walk-forward validation.

\noindent\textbf{Objective 6.} Audit coverage, leakage, class imbalance, baseline performance, and robustness so that the results are interpreted conservatively.

\section{Chapter Summary}

This chapter established the central story of the thesis. The problem is not to build a deployable trading system, but to test whether bilingual market mood contains weak directional information for EGX30 index classification. The research questions and objectives therefore focus on point-in-time coverage, sentiment-only feature construction, and strict evaluation. The next chapter introduces the background concepts needed to justify those design choices.
