\chapter{Related Work}
\label{chap:related}

This chapter reviews work that motivates the thesis and narrows the research gap. It does not treat every prior experiment as a model to copy. Instead, it asks what each line of work teaches about source choice, language, target definition, baselines, and leakage-safe evaluation.

\section{Text-Aware Financial Forecasting}

Early stock-forecasting systems relied mainly on prices, returns, volumes, and technical indicators. Text-aware forecasting expanded the feature space by adding news, social media, or event representations. AZFinText showed that breaking financial news can support short-term directional prediction when entity linkage and timing are controlled \cite{schumaker2009azfin}. Later work improved text representation through context-aware features, event embeddings, attention models, and deep fusion architectures \cite{hagenau2013automated, ding2015event, hu2018chaotic, xu2018stock}.

The lesson for this thesis is not that more complex models are always better. The lesson is that text must be linked to the correct market entity and aligned to the correct prediction horizon. A bilingual EGX30 study therefore needs a defensible corpus construction step before any model result can be interpreted.

\section{Social Media and Market Mood}

Social media is attractive because it is timely and large in volume. Studies of Twitter mood, message-board posts, and search signals report that aggregate public attention can relate to market behavior \cite{bollen2011twitter, nguyen2015social, mao2011predicting}. Arabic social-media work also suggests that Arabic investor discussion may contain information relevant to Arab market indices, including EGX30 \cite{alkhatib2016arabictweets}.

These studies are useful as evidence that public language can matter, but they do not define the main scope of this thesis. Social media creates access, noise, entity-linking, and redistribution constraints. For the final bachelor thesis, social media is therefore treated as future work, while the core study uses auditable financial-news pages.

\section{Financial News and Stock Prediction}

Financial-news studies are closer to the present problem because news articles are more structured and more likely to refer to identifiable firms. Large-scale U.S. studies combine company news with prices and deep models \cite{mohan2019news}. Other studies compare recurrent models with and without news sentiment, or fuse sentiment with chart representations \cite{dahal2023comparative, huang2021candlestick}. Index-level models such as SA-DLSTM also show how online sentiment and market variables can be integrated for broad-market movement prediction \cite{zhao2023integrated}.

These studies show that sentiment can be useful, but their settings differ from this thesis. Many use developed markets, English text, social-media sentiment, or price-derived predictors. The final EGX30 study instead asks a narrower question: whether bilingual constituent-linked financial-news sentiment alone contains directional information for an Egyptian index target.

\section{Domain-Specific and Arabic Sentiment Models}

The English side of financial sentiment draws on strong domain-specific support. FinBERT was developed for financial text and improves over generic language models on financial sentiment tasks \cite{araci2019finbert}. This makes it a suitable English route for the thesis.

The Arabic side is less settled for finance-specific sentiment. AraBERT, ARBERT, MARBERT, and CAMeLBERT provide strong Arabic representations, but their benchmark success does not automatically establish forecasting value in EGX news \cite{antoun2020arabert, abdulmageed2021arbert, inoue2021camelbert}. This motivates comparing Arabic encoder routes inside the same sentiment-only EGX30 protocol.

\section{Evaluation Practice and Baselines}

A recurring limitation in sentiment forecasting is inconsistent evaluation. Some studies report regression error, others report accuracy, and fewer report balanced accuracy or class-distribution diagnostics. Horizon-comparative evidence shows that sentiment effects are conditional on target horizon and feature setup rather than universal \cite{liapis2023deep}.

For this reason, the present thesis treats baseline comparison as part of the main evidence. Always-Up, always-Down, training-majority, rolling-majority, article-count-only, and shuffled-sentiment baselines test whether the sentiment model is doing more than following label skew or news volume. Results are interpreted primarily through balanced accuracy, not raw accuracy.

The table shows that prior work rarely combines all constraints required here. Developed-market studies have richer coverage but weaker relevance to EGX. Social-media studies are timely but noisy and difficult to audit. Arabic NLP studies justify language-specific encoders but do not test EGX30 direction classification.

The comparison also shows why the final study should not be framed as a simple application of an existing model. If the market setting is changed to Egypt, the language setting becomes bilingual. If the target is changed to EGX30, the news corpus must follow historical index membership. If price-derived predictors are removed, the model has less information but the interpretation becomes cleaner. If walk-forward validation and class-imbalance baselines are enforced, some attractive raw-accuracy results become much weaker.

Taken together, these constraints define the thesis contribution. The study combines an Egyptian index target, Arabic-English news, point-in-time constituent coverage, sentiment-only predictors, chronological validation, and baseline checks in one reproducible setup. Prior work covers many of these pieces separately, but rarely all at once.

\section{Research Gap}

The remaining gap is geographic and methodological. Most prior work does not simultaneously address the Egyptian market, bilingual Arabic-English financial news, point-in-time index constituent coverage, sentiment-only predictors, strict walk-forward validation, and class-imbalance-aware baselines.

This thesis addresses that gap by treating the evaluation setup itself as the contribution. The aim is not to claim strong market predictability. The aim is to provide a reproducible test of whether bilingual EGX30 market mood contains weak directional signal under careful validation.

\section{Chapter Summary}

This chapter reviewed prior work and narrowed the research gap. The literature supports the relevance of financial text, domain-specific sentiment models, and language-aware processing, but it also warns that sentiment effects are conditional and sensitive to evaluation design. The next chapter turns these lessons into the thesis methodology: a point-in-time, bilingual, sentiment-only EGX30 forecasting pipeline.
