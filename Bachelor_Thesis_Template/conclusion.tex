\chapter{Conclusion \& Future Work}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This final chapter summarizes what the thesis built, what the results show, and what should be done next. The conclusion remains intentionally conservative because the strongest results indicate weak directional signal, not strong market predictability.

\section{Conclusion}

This thesis built a reproducible point-in-time study of bilingual financial-news sentiment for EGX30 direction classification. The study reconstructs historical EGX30 membership and collects Arabic and English constituent-linked news. It scores sentiment with language-specific encoders, aggregates daily market mood, constructs EGX30 direction labels, and evaluates the result with walk-forward validation.

The main empirical finding is conservative. The best bilingual row, FinBERT + CAMeLBERT at the 1-trading-day horizon, reaches accuracy 0.621 and balanced accuracy 0.545. This suggests limited but non-zero directional evidence. It does not show strong market predictability, and it does not justify a trading-system claim.

A supplementary price-plus-sentiment experiment gives a stronger applied result. The lagged bilingual sentiment features are added to an EGX30 historical XGBoost model. Test accuracy improves from 0.739 to 0.802, balanced accuracy improves from 0.757 to 0.793, and F1 improves from 0.789 to 0.851. This supports the practical conclusion that sentiment is more effective as an incremental feature in a historical model than as a standalone index-direction signal.

The baseline and class-imbalance checks are central to that conclusion. Always-Up and majority baselines can achieve attractive raw accuracy because the test labels lean upward. Balanced accuracy, MCC, predicted Up share, and baseline deltas prevent the thesis from overstating the model.

The validity audit supports the evaluation protocol. It verifies target construction, lag alignment, point-in-time membership, cross-company deduplication, walk-forward history, and metric parity. The contribution is therefore best understood as a conservative evaluation framework for bilingual EGX30 market mood.

\section{Limitations}

The study uses Mubasher as its main news source, so coverage may miss information from disclosures, broker reports, or other media. Coverage is also uneven across constituents even after point-in-time scoping. Some companies receive substantially more articles than others.

The sentiment labels come from off-the-shelf encoders rather than expert-labeled Egyptian financial sentiment data. FinBERT is finance-specific for English. The Arabic route depends on general Arabic encoders, which limits how confidently the thesis can interpret Arabic sentiment quality.

The main model is intentionally sentiment-only. This isolates the text signal, but it also means the main benchmark should not be compared directly with full trading models that use prices, technical indicators, or macro variables. The supplementary XGBoost integration addresses this comparison only for one historical-model family and one target definition.

\section{Future Work}

Future work should start with expert-labeled Arabic financial sentiment data. Such a dataset would allow direct supervised evaluation of Arabic encoders in the financial domain instead of relying only on general Arabic sentiment models.

A second direction is richer event and materiality annotation. The current thesis uses sentiment and coverage signals, but it does not attempt complex event extraction. Future work could distinguish earnings, dividends, financing, regulation, governance, and contracts under a separately validated annotation scheme.

Broader auditable sources would also strengthen the protocol. Official EGX disclosures, company filings, and carefully archived news providers could improve coverage. True historical index weights should be added only if they become available from a reproducible source.

Finally, the successful XGBoost integration should be expanded into a broader price-only, sentiment-only, and price-plus-sentiment comparison under one unified point-in-time and walk-forward protocol. Social-media data should be considered only through approved access, careful entity filtering, and clear research-data handling rules.

Overall, the thesis shows that EGX30 sentiment forecasting should be judged through careful validation rather than raw accuracy alone. The measured signal is weak, but the evaluation setup is reusable and auditable. This makes the work a foundation for future Egyptian-market sentiment studies rather than a final trading model.
