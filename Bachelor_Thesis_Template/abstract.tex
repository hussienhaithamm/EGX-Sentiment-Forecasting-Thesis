\chapter*{Abstract}
% \addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\label{chap:abstract}
This thesis will investigate whether bilingual financial-news sentiment can support short-horizon stock direction forecasting on the Egyptian Exchange (EGX). The empirical scope will cover three EGX equities: Commercial International Bank (CIB), Talaat Moustafa Group (TMG), and Orascom Investment Holding (OIH). The article corpus will be drawn from Mubasher company pages and will include 1{,}921 bilingual articles after cleaning, with 1{,}335 Arabic items and 586 English items spanning a multi-year window. The motivation will be an industrial brief from Robin, the data-science arm of Beltone Holding, which will frame the question of whether sentiment-derived signals will add forecasting value beyond historical price data while remaining interpretable and reproducible.

The pipeline will collect bilingual articles, route English text to FinBERT and Arabic text to an AraBERT-based sentiment route, weight each article by ticker-match confidence and model confidence, aggregate article-level scores into one-day-lagged daily features, and evaluate all-news, event-only, and event-weighted representations under chronological walk-forward validation. The final sentiment-only benchmark will use logistic regression for direction prediction and ridge regression for future-return prediction at horizons of 1, 3, and 5 trading days. Leakage, coverage, and overfitting audits will form part of the experimental design rather than serve as optional post-processing.

The reporting plan will prefer conservative, validity-passing results over the highest raw accuracy. The accepted final candidate will be reported per ticker, horizon, and feature view together with balanced accuracy, F1, return sign accuracy, and the train-validation-test gap that justifies acceptance. Four auxiliary experiments will accompany the main benchmark: a historical-model integration test with sentiment as an added feature, a Spearman correlation study with five-day block-permutation p-values, an Arabic financial-domain Masked Language Modeling (MLM) adaptation of AraBERT measured by held-out MLM perplexity (this measures domain language modeling fit, not supervised sentiment performance, which will remain future work pending Arabic financial sentiment labels), and a social-media access feasibility audit covering Reddit and X/Twitter.

The methodological contribution will be a reproducible, language-aware, and leakage-safe protocol for testing bilingual financial-news sentiment in a sparse emerging-market setting. All scripts, data manifests, evaluation outputs, and audit reports will be released through a public repository so that every reported number can be regenerated from the same configuration.
