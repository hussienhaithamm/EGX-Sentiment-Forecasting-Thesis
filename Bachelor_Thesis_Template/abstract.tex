\chapter*{Abstract}
\label{chap:abstract}

This thesis studies whether bilingual Arabic-English financial news contains short-horizon directional information for the Egyptian Exchange EGX30 index. The setting is an emerging-market information environment where firm-related news appears in both Arabic and English, and where using the current EGX30 list retrospectively would create survivorship bias.

The study reconstructs a point-in-time (PIT) EGX30 constituent universe from 21 April 2021 to 20 April 2026. The final registry contains 12 continuous membership intervals and a historical union of 57 companies. Arabic and English Mubasher articles linked to those historical constituents are collected, normalized, filtered by active membership, deduplicated across company pages, and scored with language-specific sentiment models. English articles are scored with FinBERT, while the Arabic route compares AraBERT and CAMeLBERT.

The primary forecasting design is intentionally sentiment-only. EGX30 historical closes are used only to construct 1-, 3-, and 5-trading-day direction labels, and no closes, returns, Open, High, Low, Close, Volume (OHLCV) fields, technical indicators, or other price-derived variables enter the main model matrix. Daily company sentiment is aggregated into an equal-weight market-mood signal, shifted before trading-date alignment, and evaluated with strict expanding-window walk-forward validation.

The strongest bilingual test row by balanced accuracy is FinBERT + CAMeLBERT at the 1-trading-day horizon, with accuracy 0.621, balanced accuracy 0.545, F1 0.737, and Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) 0.115. Baseline and class-imbalance checks show that raw accuracy is partly explained by majority-class behavior, and only 8 of 18 sentiment-model test rows beat the training-majority baseline on balanced accuracy. A supplementary XGBoost integration experiment adds the same lagged sentiment features to an EGX30 historical OHLCV model; accuracy improves from 0.739 to 0.802 and balanced accuracy improves from 0.757 to 0.793. A validity audit passes target recomputation, lag alignment, point-in-time membership, cross-company deduplication, walk-forward history, and metric-parity checks.

The results provide promising but limited evidence of weak directional signal in bilingual EGX30 market mood. The contribution is a reproducible and conservative protocol for testing bilingual market mood in an emerging market, rather than a deployable trading system.
