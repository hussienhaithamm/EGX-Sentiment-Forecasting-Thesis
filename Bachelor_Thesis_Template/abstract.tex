\chapter*{Abstract}
% \addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\label{chap:abstract}
This thesis investigates whether bilingual financial-news sentiment can support short-horizon stock direction forecasting for selected Egyptian Exchange equities. The study focuses on Commercial International Bank, Talaat Moustafa Group, and Orascom Investment Holding, using Arabic and English company-linked news from Mubasher. The work was motivated by an industrial research question from Robin, the data-science arm of Beltone Holding: whether sentiment signals can add predictive value beyond historical market data while remaining interpretable and reproducible.

The implemented pipeline collects and cleans bilingual news, routes English articles to FinBERT and Arabic articles to an AraBERT-based sentiment route, weights article sentiment by relevance and confidence, aggregates article-level predictions into lagged daily features, and evaluates all-news, event-only, and event-weighted representations under chronological walk-forward validation. The final sentiment-only benchmark uses logistic regression for direction prediction and ridge regression for future-return prediction across 1, 3, and 5 trading-day horizons. Leakage, coverage, and overfitting checks are treated as part of the experimental design rather than as optional post-processing.

The main result is intentionally conservative. The strongest accepted sentiment-only candidate is OIH at the 5-day horizon under the all-news bilingual setting, with test accuracy 0.532, balanced accuracy 0.503, F1 score 0.630, and return sign accuracy 0.585. Event-aware variants produced isolated gains but did not improve performance consistently. A separate historical-model integration experiment showed that adding sentiment to a price-based EGX forecasting model improved raw accuracy in 5 of 9 ticker-model comparisons, but the gains were conditional rather than uniform. A sentiment-return correlation study found no stable monotonic association under the pre-specified threshold. An Arabic financial AraBERT adaptation experiment reduced held-out masked-language-model loss by 12.01\% relative to base AraBERT, supporting domain-adaptation feasibility but not supervised sentiment superiority. Finally, a social-media feasibility study concluded that Reddit and X/Twitter should remain future work unless official approved access is available.

Overall, the thesis finds that bilingual financial-news sentiment contains weak and unstable information for EGX forecasting in the current dataset. Its strongest contribution is therefore methodological: a reproducible, language-aware, leakage-safe framework for testing sentiment signals honestly in a sparse emerging-market setting.
