\section{Main Multi-Horizon Results}

The final accepted run evaluates three forecast horizons: 1, 3, and 5 trading days. Direction prediction uses logistic regression with balanced class weights, while return prediction uses ridge regression. Features are standardised before model fitting. The classification regularisation parameter is tuned over the grid $\{0.05, 0.1, 0.5\}$. The ridge configuration uses $\alpha = 10.0$. Classification thresholds are not fixed at 0.5. They are selected from validation predictions only and applied unchanged to the test block. The chronological setup matches Chapter~\ref{chap:methodology}, while the implementation adds the concrete numerical settings already listed in Table~\ref{tab:exp_setup}.

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports the final all-news bilingual results that passed the chronological evaluation pipeline. Table~\ref{tab:main_results} shows a weak and inconsistent sentiment-only signal. The best accepted ticker-horizon pair is OIH at the 5-day horizon, with test accuracy 0.425, balanced accuracy 0.503, F1 0.021, sign accuracy 0.585, and return correlation 0.026. The best balanced accuracy in the final accepted run appears for TMG at the 1-day horizon, where balanced accuracy reaches 0.506. These values are above random only marginally. They do not support a claim that bilingual news sentiment alone is a strong standalone EGX forecasting model.

Another pattern is that F1 can look stronger than balanced accuracy. This occurs when the model predicts the majority upward class more often than the minority downward class. Several rows show F1 above 0.60 while balanced accuracy remains close to 0.50. Balanced accuracy is therefore more informative than raw accuracy or F1 alone in an imbalanced directional setting.

\section{Event Filtering and Event Weighting}

The thesis also evaluates whether event filtering improves the signal. On aggregate, it marginally does. Relative to the all-news bilingual baseline, the mean event-only accuracy change is $+0.025$ and the mean event-weighted accuracy change is $+0.032$. The event-aware variants produce a few locally interesting cases. CIB improves from 0.487 to 0.499 accuracy at the 1-day horizon under the event-only setting, and from 0.510 to 0.525 at the 3-day horizon. OIH at the 5-day horizon improves from 0.425 to 0.563 accuracy and from 0.021 to 0.700 F1 under event weighting. These gains are driven by a small number of large improvements: 7 of 9 ticker-horizon pairs show positive accuracy deltas under both strategies, but the magnitude is highly concentrated in a few cases. The broader conclusion is that event information helps selectively, and the aggregate positive mean is carried largely by OIH at the 5-day horizon, which also shows the highest overfitting risk.

\section{Validity Checks and Accepted Final Model}

The strongest raw-accuracy candidate observed in the final multi-horizon event run was OIH at the 5-day horizon under the event-weighted bilingual setting. Test accuracy reached 0.563 and F1 reached 0.700. The corresponding row was flagged by the Phase 7 validity analysis because training accuracy rose to 0.609 while validation accuracy fell to 0.485. The result is therefore exploratory rather than accepted.

The accepted final candidate is OIH at the 5-day horizon under the all-news bilingual setting. Its validation accuracy, balanced accuracy, and F1 are 0.524, 0.501, and 0.057. Its test accuracy, balanced accuracy, F1, and sign accuracy are 0.425, 0.503, 0.021, and 0.585. This candidate is retained because it passes the leakage and overfitting filters and its validation balanced accuracy clears the minimum acceptance threshold of 0.490. The near-zero F1 on both validation and test indicates that the logistic regression threshold selected from validation predictions pushes the classifier heavily toward one class; sign accuracy (0.585), which does not depend on the classification threshold, remains the more informative metric for this candidate.

The Phase 7 validity results quantify the reliability of the full pipeline. Leakage checks pass for 45 panel evaluations covering 43{,}880 validation and test prediction rows, and no history violations are detected. Coverage analysis shows that TMG and OIH match the currently available Mubasher listing pages, while CIB has one additional Arabic listing page available at audit time. Overfitting analysis flags 14 of the 45 experiment rows, which confirms that raw test accuracy alone would overstate the quality of the strongest-looking candidate.

Coverage remains a substantive limitation. Prior-7-day article coverage is 0.569 for CIB, 0.387 for TMG, and 0.307 for OIH. These values explain part of the instability seen in the ticker-level results. The weakest panels are not simply weaker models. They are weaker information histories.

\section{Auxiliary Experiments and Research Journey}

The final sentiment-only benchmark was not the first experimental form of the project. The research process began with the industrial question of whether a sentiment signal improves an existing EGX direction-forecasting model based on historical prices and technical features. That historical integration experiment uses the Robin/Beltone internship codebase as an external baseline and compares the original historical features against the same feature set augmented with one-day-lagged bilingual sentiment features. The experiment runs for CIB, TMG, and OIH using logistic regression, XGBoost, and a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) under a shared chronological test period.

The historical integration result is mixed. Sentiment improves raw test accuracy in 5 of 9 ticker-model comparisons. The largest gain appears for OIH with MLP: accuracy increases by 0.143, balanced accuracy by 0.106, and F1 by 0.309. The largest loss appears for CIB with MLP: accuracy decreases by 0.105. Logistic regression is mostly flat or weaker after sentiment is added. XGBoost shows small gains for CIB and OIH and a small loss for TMG. The result is useful as motivation but does not cleanly isolate the value of sentiment because the baseline already contains many price-derived predictors. That limitation is the reason the thesis moves toward the stricter sentiment-only benchmark reported above.

A second auxiliary experiment inspects the raw relationship between daily news sentiment and stock returns. Daily bilingual sentiment is shifted by one calendar day before alignment to trading dates, then correlated with same-day, 1-day, 3-day, and 5-day returns. Pearson and Spearman correlations are computed, and p-values are estimated by block permutation with five-day blocks. No ticker-horizon pair for the main lagged weighted-sentiment feature meets the pre-specified threshold of absolute Spearman correlation at least 0.10 and permutation p-value at most 0.05. The strongest displayed associations remain weak: OIH 3-day Spearman correlation is $-0.127$ with p-value 0.155, OIH 5-day is $-0.109$ with p-value 0.186, and TMG same-day is $-0.102$ with p-value 0.120. This supports a conservative interpretation. Sentiment shows weak descriptive associations but not a stable monotonic return relation by itself.

The Arabic sentiment component is also tested from a domain-adaptation perspective. Following the logic of FinBERT, a valid Arabic financial analogue requires finance-domain language-model adaptation followed by supervised sentiment fine-tuning on independent human-labeled Arabic financial examples. The available thesis corpus supports only the first stage. The AraBERT domain-adaptation run used 1{,}327 Arabic financial articles available at training time, split chronologically into 1{,}061 Masked Language Modeling (MLM) training articles and 266 validation articles. A short CPU-based AraBERT adaptation run completes for 20 steps, with training loss 0.9676 and validation loss 0.8218 measured during training. A follow-up fixed-mask evaluation compares the adapted checkpoint against the original AraBERT under identical conditions: the same chronological 80/20 validation split, the same fixed seed of 42 across Python, NumPy, and PyTorch, the same base AraBERT tokenizer for both runs, and the identical evaluation script. Table~\ref{tab:arabert_mlm_eval} reports the resulting per-split MLM loss and perplexity on held-out Arabic financial news text. This measures domain language modelling fit, not supervised sentiment performance, which remains future work pending Arabic financial sentiment labels. Supervised Arabic financial sentiment fine-tuning is excluded because the present labels are model-generated outputs from the current AraBERT route and do not constitute independent human annotations \cite{araci2019finbert, antoun2020arabert}.

The final auxiliary investigation considers social-media sources. The requirement is a free, ethical, and officially approved route for EGX social-media sentiment. The conclusion is restrictive. Reddit is considered only through official API or approved research access and with limits on redistribution and model training. X/Twitter is excluded from the current implementation path because broad public-read access is paid under the official API model, while unofficial scraping is inappropriate for an academic thesis. Prior work on Arabic tweets and Arab stock-market indices, including EGX30, supports the relevance of this direction without removing the current access constraint \cite{alkhatib2016arabictweets}.

\section{Interpretation}

The final evidence supports a limited rather than optimistic conclusion. Bilingual news sentiment contains some directional information. The signal is not stable enough to serve as a strong standalone EGX forecasting system in the present dataset. The strongest accepted result is only marginally above random under balanced accuracy. Several apparently strong F1 values are driven by class skew rather than by robust up-versus-down discrimination.

The mixed result is still informative for two reasons. First, it justifies the conservative methodology because looser reporting would have selected the overfit OIH event-weighted model as the headline result. Second, it clarifies where later work should focus: richer relevance modelling, denser news coverage, Arabic financial-domain adaptation with human sentiment labels, official social-media access, and eventual integration with price-derived features rather than sentiment-only forecasting.

\section{Chapter Summary}

This chapter reported the implementation settings and the empirical results. The benchmark uses bilingual Mubasher news, simple walk-forward models with leakage controls, and explicit validity checks. The best accepted result is OIH at the 5-day horizon, but its balanced accuracy stays only slightly above random. Event filtering shows a small positive average gain, though it is concentrated in a few panels. The auxiliary experiments round out the picture: sentiment helps historical models in some cases, the raw sentiment-return correlation is weak, AraBERT domain adaptation lowers held-out MLM loss, and social media remains out of scope without official access. The next chapter draws the overall conclusions and points to future directions.
